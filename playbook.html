Session 1 · 90 min

Find the Truth

Before any marketing or growth tactic, you need to know whether the product actually delivers value, for whom, at what price. Sessions that skip this stage build growth machines that amplify the wrong product to the wrong people.

1.1 PMF & User Research

"Product market fit is something where you absolutely know it when you see it. Therefore if you don't absolutely know it, you don't have it."— Matt MacInnis

PMF is not a binary switch. It is a spectrum that moves through four levels:

Level 1Problem Fit — Real people have the problem you're solving. You've confirmed demand without building anything.
Level 2Solution Fit — Your solution solves the problem. Users who try it get value and say so.
Level 3Product Fit — Your product reliably delivers that solution at scale. Onboarding works. Retention is positive.
Level 4Market Fit — A large enough segment buys repeatedly. CAC is recoverable. NRR exceeds 100%. The machine works.
The Sean Ellis TestSurvey active users: "How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?" If <40% answer "Very disappointed", you do not have PMF. Run by segment — you may have PMF with one ICP but not another. HeyGen scored >40% among enterprise comms teams but not among casual video creators.

PMF Loss Signals — Watch for These

  • Rising churn despite growing acquisition — the bucket leaks faster than you fill it
  • Support tickets cluster around the same 2–3 failure points — onboarding is broken
  • NPS is flat or declining quarter-over-quarter despite product updates
  • Sales cycle extends — buyers need more convincing, which means value proposition is unclear
  • Users who were "activated" in month 1 don't return in month 3
Case Study
HeyGen: Narrowing ICP to Find PMF

HeyGen launched as an AI video creation tool for "anyone who makes videos." They had thousands of users but inconsistent retention. The breakthrough came when they ran Sean Ellis tests segmented by use case. Enterprise comms teams — those creating multilingual training and marketing videos — scored 67% "Very disappointed." Casual creators scored 18%. HeyGen rebuilt messaging and onboarding exclusively around the enterprise use case, tripling retention among the segment that mattered. By 2024 they were at $35M ARR.

Lesson: Broad ICP = diluted value. Narrow until the Sean Ellis score exceeds 40% in a segment large enough to build a business on.

User Interview SOP

5-Step Interview Protocol
  1. Recruit the right people — current users, lapsed users, and non-users who match your ICP. Never friends.
  2. Start with context, not product — "Walk me through the last time you dealt with [problem]." You want to understand their world before showing yours.
  3. Probe the status quo — "What do you do today when this happens? What do you hate about it?"
  4. Test willingness to pay (WTP) — Van Westendorp price sensitivity meter: too cheap / bargain / getting expensive / too expensive. You want the range between "bargain" and "getting expensive."
  5. Synthesize across 8–10 interviews — look for pattern convergence in Dovetail or Notion, not individual outliers.

1.2 Positioning & Pricing

"Weak positioning hurts you in the early stages of pipeline — people don't get what you are."— April Dunford

Positioning answers one question in the buyer's mind: why this, instead of that? Most startups describe what their product does (features). April Dunford's framework forces you to describe what makes it uniquely better for a specific buyer vs. a specific alternative.

April Dunford's 5-Component Positioning Framework
  1. Competitive alternatives — what would buyers do if your product didn't exist? (Excel, hiring someone, a competitor, doing nothing)
  2. Unique attributes — what do you have that alternatives don't?
  3. Value (not features) — what value do those attributes unlock for the buyer?
  4. Segmentation — who cares most about that specific value? That's your best-fit ICP.
  5. Market category — what frame do you put around yourself? The wrong category makes you look expensive or irrelevant.
Case Study
Supabase: Positioning Against Firebase

Firebase was the dominant backend-as-a-service for developers. Supabase positioned not as "another BaaS" but as "the open-source Firebase alternative." Their tagline: "Build in a weekend. Scale to millions." This single line collapsed the complexity objection (scale is hard) while owning the speed advantage (weekend = fast). By naming the competitor in their positioning, they captured every developer searching for "Firebase alternative" on Google — and converted them with open-source credibility.

Lesson: Naming your competitive alternative in positioning is not weakness — it's the fastest path to being understood by the right buyer.

Pricing Principles

Principle 1Price is a value signal. Pricing too low communicates low quality. Price at what the segment believes the value is worth, not at cost + margin.
Principle 2Freemium gates the wrong thing. Most teams gate premium features. Masters gate scale — free up to X, paid above X. This converts power users, not casual browsers.
Principle 3Self-serve ceiling exists. Deals above $25–50K ACV almost always need a human in the loop. Build the PLS motion (product-led sales) before you hit the ceiling.
Principle 4Reverse trial works. Give users the paid tier free for 14 days, then downgrade. Converts 2–3x better than free tier → upgrade, because loss aversion beats feature discovery.

Session 2 · 90 min

Tell the Story — Brand & Awareness

Brand is not a logo. It is a coherent promise that makes one type of person feel seen and makes everyone else self-select out. The best brands in tech aren't neutral — they have a point of view that attracts passionate advocates and repels the wrong buyers.

2.1 Founder Brand

"Attention is the ultimate currency. Authenticity beats polish. Founders must own their narrative."— Lulu Cheng Meservey

A founder with 100K followers in the right niche is worth more than $200K in ad spend. Founder brand is a compounding distribution asset — every post extends reach for free, builds trust faster than any ad, and creates inbound that no SDR can replicate.

Platform Playbooks

LinkedInTone: Professional insight with personality. What works: contrarian takes on industry norms, process breakdowns ("here's how we closed our Series A"), specific numbers ("we went from 0 to 10K users in 8 weeks — here's what actually drove it"). Cadence: 3–4 posts/week. DM reply rate: 10–15%.
Twitter / XTone: Direct, sharp, opinionated. What works: threads with 10–15 specific lessons, hot takes on product, live build-in-public updates. Cadence: 1–2 posts/day. First 60 seconds after posting determine algorithmic reach — reply to every early comment.
XHS (小红书)Tone: Lifestyle + insight hybrid. What works: "a day in the life of a founder" formats, product use in real-life contexts, emotional storytelling with product integration. Cadence: 4–5 posts/week. Discovery is keyword-driven — use exact-match keyword phrases in first line.
Hook BankStart with: "Most [category] advice is wrong. Here's what actually works." / "I spent $200K on ads before I learned this one thing." / "Nobody talks about this but..." / "After [X] years, here's the uncomfortable truth." / "We [achieved result]. Here's the system."
Case Study
Gong: Category Creation Through Thought Leadership

Gong's CEO Amit Bendov didn't just sell conversation intelligence — he created the category of "Revenue Intelligence." Every LinkedIn post, every webinar, every piece of content was positioned around this new frame. Their "State of Revenue Intelligence" annual report became industry-required reading, cited by VCs and sales leaders. They controlled the category definition, so any competitor was automatically "a Gong alternative." This positioning contributed to a $7.25B valuation.

Lesson: The company that names the category wins the category. Create the frame, then own it through consistent thought leadership.

2.2 Storytelling & PR

"Never fight a story with a statistic. Always fight story with story."— Lulu Cheng Meservey

The Go Direct Framework

Traditional PR waits for media coverage. Go Direct means the company publishes its own narrative through owned channels before, during, and after any media coverage. When journalists do write about you, they pull from your framing.

4 PR Narrative Structures
  1. Origin story — "We built this because we faced the problem ourselves." Authenticity + urgency. (Airbnb: "We rented air mattresses to pay rent and accidentally built a company.")
  2. Foil story — "The way everyone does this is wrong, and here's why." Creates tension, positions you as the smart alternative. (Notion: "Spreadsheets aren't databases. Docs aren't wikis. We're both.")
  3. Movement story — "We're not selling a product, we're starting a movement." Bigger than you. (Patagonia: "We're in business to save our home planet.")
  4. Data story — "Here's a trend only we can see because of our data." Credibility + exclusivity. (Gong's sales data reports, Stripe's developer economy reports.)
Case Study
Airbnb: Storytelling as Survival

During COVID, Airbnb hosts were facing financial ruin. Instead of a corporate press release, Airbnb CEO Brian Chesky wrote an open letter to employees when they had to lay off 25% of the team — with radical transparency about severance, healthcare, and why it happened. The letter went viral. Journalists wrote about it as an example of exceptional leadership. During one of the worst crises in travel history, Airbnb built more brand trust than most companies do in a decade. Their IPO in December 2020 — despite $700M losses — was oversubscribed.

Lesson: Authentic storytelling in a crisis builds more trust than a polished statement. The vulnerability is the story.

2.3 SEO & GEO (AI Search Optimization)

SEO in 2025: Two Parallel Engines

Traditional SEO (Google Search) still drives enormous volume, but GEO — Generative Engine Optimization — is rapidly becoming a parallel channel. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude synthesize answers from web content. Being cited in those answers requires different tactics than ranking in Google.

Traditional SEOPriority keywords: Bottom-of-funnel first ("best CRM for startups"). Content type: Comparison pages, alternative pages, how-to guides. Authority building: Backlinks from domain-relevant sites. Case: HubSpot's 500+ comparison pages drive 30% of their trial signups.
GEO (AI Search)What works: Original data and research (AI cites sources). Comprehensive definitional articles. FAQ content that answers exactly what AI models are asked. Format: Clear headings, structured data. Monitoring: Profound, Otterly.ai — track when AI answers cite your domain.
Domain HacksSmallPDF bought expired high-authority domains and redirected 301s to their content. This transferred decades of domain authority overnight. Works for any content-heavy product. Investment: $2–20K. Return: equivalent to 2 years of organic link building.
Topical AuthorityDon't scatter content across 50 topics. Pick 3–5 core topics and dominate them. Notion owns "project management templates," "wiki software," and "team collaboration" as topic clusters. Google now rewards clusters over individual articles.
Case Study
HubSpot: The Content Machine That Built a $20B Company

HubSpot's content strategy started in 2006 — years before "content marketing" was a term. They published educational content on inbound marketing (a term they coined), building topical authority around their product category. By the time competitors arrived, HubSpot owned thousands of keywords across the marketing funnel. Today, their blog receives 7M+ monthly visits. Most visitors are in problem-awareness mode — perfect for building brand trust before they consider buying. HubSpot's organic search alone is valued at $500M+ in equivalent paid traffic.

Lesson: The earlier you start building topical authority, the more compounding returns you accumulate. SEO is a content endowment, not a campaign.

2.4 Social Search (TikTok / Instagram as Search Engine)

Gen Z uses TikTok before Google for recommendations, how-tos, and product discovery. For consumer products, social search is now the primary discovery channel for the 18–34 demographic.

5-Dimension Viability AuditBefore investing in social search for a client: (1) Search volume — do people actually search this topic on TikTok? (2) Content competition — how many creators are in this niche? (3) Creator saturation — are there already 100+ creators with 100K+ followers? (4) Monetization fit — does the niche attract buyers, not just browsers? (5) Brand safety — can you show up authentically without brand risk?
Case Study
Duolingo: The Brand That Learned to Troll Itself

Duolingo's TikTok strategy is case-study-famous. Their green owl mascot "Duo" was reframed as a passive-aggressive stalker reminding users to practice. The account accumulated 7M+ followers not through product demos but through absurdist humor that went viral. Crucially, every video still embedded a behavioral nudge — "you haven't practiced today." The result: TikTok drove a 50%+ increase in app downloads during their peak viral moments, all at zero paid media cost.

Lesson: On TikTok, entertainment and product message must coexist. Find the tension or humor in your product's core mechanic and dramatize it.

Session 3 · 90 min

Light the Fire — User Acquisition

Acquisition is only valuable if the users you bring in stay. Before scaling any channel, confirm your activation and D7 retention hit benchmarks. Otherwise you're accelerating churn, not growth.

3.1 Launch Playbook

A launch is not a day — it's a 6-week campaign with the "launch day" in the middle. The pre-launch builds pressure; the post-launch converts momentum into users and press.

6-Week Launch SOP
Weeks -6 to -4Build the audience: Founder posts daily on target platforms. Identify and brief 10–20 KOLs. Create waitlist with referral mechanic (GrowSurf, Viral Loops). Seed private communities (Slack groups, Discord servers) with early access offer.
Weeks -3 to -2Warm the machine: Ship teaser content (behind-the-scenes, problems-we-solve series). Send KOL assets and brief. Post Product Hunt "upcoming" page. Collect testimonials from beta users. Prepare PR pitch for 5 target journalists.
Launch Week5-day drop structure (Supabase model): Day 1: flagship feature. Day 2: integration or partnership. Day 3: customer story. Day 4: data / research release. Day 5: community event or AMA. Each day is its own announcement.
Weeks +1 to +2Convert the momentum: Follow up with all waitlist signups within 24h. Run retargeting ads to site visitors from launch week. Publish launch retrospective (transparency builds trust). Submit to Product Hunt if not already done.

Product Hunt Strategy

PH Full StrategyHunter selection: Find a hunter with 3K+ followers and past Top 5 launches. DM them 4 weeks out. Comment velocity: First 2 hours are critical. Have 50+ supporters lined up to comment (not vote) immediately at 12:01am PT. KOL coordination: Ask each KOL to post their Product Hunt link to their audience at launch time. Timing: Tuesday–Thursday launches outperform Monday and Friday. Launch at 12:01am PT.
Case Study
Linear: The Invite-Only Launch That Created FOMO

Linear launched in 2020 with a tiny team and zero paid marketing. They made the product invite-only — not as a genuine technical constraint but as a positioning move. The scarcity created organic buzz on Twitter among developers. Early access felt like joining an exclusive club. Their design-forward approach and keyboard-first product generated obsessive word-of-mouth. The waitlist grew to 30,000+ before public launch. By keeping the product hard to access, they turned the launch itself into marketing. Linear was valued at $400M in their 2022 funding round.

Lesson: Artificial scarcity converts curious observers into active advocates. The waitlist is not just a queue — it's your first community.
Case Study
Superhuman: Qualification as Brand Signal

Superhuman's waitlist didn't just collect emails — it asked qualifying questions: "What email client do you use? How many emails do you send per day? How would you rate your inbox management?" Two effects: (1) They collected ICP data before launch. (2) The qualification process itself made Superhuman feel premium. People who passed felt like they had earned access. At $30/month for an email app, the psychological framing of earned access justified the price. Waitlist: 275,000 people at peak.

Lesson: The application process IS the brand experience. Qualification communicates exclusivity better than any copywriting.

3.2 Growth Engines: PLG / SLG / CLG / MLG

Growth Loop Types — How Each Compounds
PLG and CLG produce compounding returns. SLG and MLG require ongoing investment but are more predictable.
Case Study
Notion: PLG to CLG to Enterprise — The Full Journey

Notion launched in 2018 with a PLG motion: free tier, personal use, beautiful product. The "personal to team" expansion was engineered into the product — you create something great, want to share it with a colleague, and suddenly a team workspace gets created. CLG amplified this: Notion's ambassador program seeded college campuses with power users who became evangelists. By 2021, bottoms-up adoption had reached enterprise decision-makers who were already using Notion personally. Enterprise sales was almost pulling, not pushing. Notion reached $10B valuation without a traditional sales-led motion until well after their CLG had saturated the market.

Lesson: PLG seeds the individual. CLG builds the social proof. Enterprise sales harvests the installed base. The sequence matters.

3.4 Mobile App Growth

Mobile app growth follows a strict prerequisite chain: ASO first (optimize the listing) → then paid UA → then retention optimization. Paying for installs before the listing is optimized is burning money. Paying for installs before D7 retention is proven is building a leaky bucket.

8-Week Mobile Launch Timeline
Weeks 1–2ASO audit: optimize title, subtitle, keywords, screenshots, and icon. Submit to TestFlight / closed beta. Collect 50+ reviews before launch to prime algorithm.
Weeks 3–4Soft launch in 2–3 lower-CPM markets (e.g. Philippines, South Africa). Optimize onboarding until D1 ≥ 30%. Fix crashes. Tune push notification permissions prompt placement.
Weeks 5–6Begin Apple Search Ads (branded + category keywords). Layer in creator UGC briefs. Set up Branch deep links for social ads → app store.
Weeks 7–8Full paid UA launch: scale Apple Search Ads, Google UAC, Meta. A/B test creatives (kill at $50 spend, scale winners). Apply for featuring with platform team.
Case Study
Calm: Scaling to $2B Without a Traditional Sales Team

Calm grew to 100M+ downloads primarily through three channels. First, Apple Health integration — Calm appeared in Apple's recommended wellness apps, giving them zero-cost distribution inside iOS. Second, ASO dominance: they owned "meditation," "sleep sounds," and "anxiety relief" on both App Stores through relentless keyword optimization and 500K+ 5-star reviews. Third, celebrity voice content (Matthew McConaughey, Harry Styles narrating sleep stories) created PR hooks that traditional wellness apps couldn't afford to match. Each celebrity signing generated earned media worth 10× the talent cost.

Lesson: Platform partnerships (Apple Health) provide distribution that no ad budget can replicate. Get native to the platform before paying for acquisition.

Build the Engine — Retention & Monetization

Retention is the truth test of whether your product creates value. All acquisition, all brand, all launch effort only compounds if users come back.

"Retention. If you create value, they will come back. If they're not coming back, you're not creating value."— Uri Levine, Co-founder of Waze

4.1 Retention & Engagement

The Aha Moment

The Aha Moment is the first time a new user experiences the core value of your product. Everything in onboarding should be engineered to deliver this moment as fast as possible.

Case Study
Spotify Discover Weekly: Personalization as Retention

Spotify had a retention problem in 2014 — users would listen for a while, then churn to YouTube or Pandora. Discover Weekly, launched in 2015, changed this. Every Monday, a playlist of 30 songs was generated specifically for each user based on listening history. The personalization was so good that users felt the product "knew them." More importantly, it gave users a reason to open Spotify every single Monday — a weekly engagement ritual. First month: 1.7M shares. After 10 weeks: 40M people had listened. Discover Weekly is now cited as the key inflection point in Spotify's retention and subscriber conversion.

Lesson: A personalized recurring ritual (weekly, daily, monthly) creates behavioral grooves that competitors cannot replicate without your data.

Mounting Loss Design

The Principle"The product should get better the more you use it, and you'll have more to lose by leaving it." — Sarah Tavel. Examples: Notion (all your work is there), Spotify (music taste is mapped), Strava (workout history, friend network). Design features that accumulate value over time so the switching cost grows with usage.

4.4 Partnerships & BD

5 BD Anti-Patterns to Avoid
  1. Partnership as press release. Announce a partnership with no activation plan. Users never experience it. Lasts 6 weeks then dies.
  2. Equal effort assumption. Assuming the larger partner will promote as hard as you will. They won't. You need to run the activation.
  3. Integration without adoption. Building a Zapier integration that 12 people use. Integrations need their own go-to-market.
  4. VC as BD shortcut. Asking investors for intros before you have a clear ask, a working product, and proof of value. Burns the intro.
  5. Co-marketing with misaligned ICP. Partnering with a company that reaches a totally different buyer. All effort, no conversion.

Warm Intro Strategy

D1 retention is the single most important metric in mobile gaming. Every 10 percentage point improvement in D1 compounds across every install you ever acquire. If D1 = 30% vs 40%, you've permanently written off 25% of your entire future LTV at scale. Fix D1 before scaling UA.
02
Seed 20 micro-streamers before seeding 1 macro-streamer. A micro-streamer (20K followers) who loves your game will post 10 videos per month for 3 months. A mega-streamer (5M followers) will post once. The micro volume wins.
03
The Live Ops calendar is a growth strategy, not a content schedule. Plan events around real-world cultural moments (holidays, sports finals, viral trends). Clash of Clans saw 40% DAU spikes during seasonal events. The event is a re-acquisition mechanism for lapsed players.
04
Design one "clip moment" per session. Ask: "What would a player want to post to TikTok / Twitch today?" If the answer is "nothing," your session loop needs work. Spectacular failure moments, rare drops, and surprise reveals are all clip-worthy by design.
05
Battle Pass economics beat IAP economics. A $10 Battle Pass generates more revenue than a $10 IAP purchase because it creates a 30-60 day engagement commitment alongside the revenue. Players who buy the Battle Pass play 2–3x more. More play = more social sharing = more organic UA.
06
The best community managers are former players, not PR people. Hire from the community. Community members who become staff have authentic credibility that no PR hire can replicate. They know what the players care about before they have to ask.
Deep Case Study
Among Us: Accidental Viral + Intentional Retention

Among Us was released in 2018 by a 3-person studio called InnerSloth. It got 30–50 daily players for 2 years. In August 2020, two Twitch streamers — Sodapoppin and xQc — started playing it to fill dead time during a stream. Within 48 hours, it had 500,000 concurrent players. The studio had not changed the game. The product was the same. What changed was a single distribution event. InnerSloth's response is the masterclass. No monetization panic: They did not rush to add pay-to-win mechanics or flood the game with ads to capitalize on the spike. They invested in servers to handle the load first. Cosmetics monetization: They released cosmetic DLC packs at $3–$5 each. No impact on gameplay fairness. Players bought because they loved the community, not because they needed an advantage. Community feedback loop: They added colorblind mode, reported bugs the community flagged, and communicated transparently about their small team's capacity. The honesty built loyalty. The result: Among Us reached 100M+ downloads and 60M daily active users at peak. The total paid marketing budget during the viral period: $0.

Master move: When a viral spike hits, serve the existing community first — don't exploit them. InnerSloth's restraint preserved community trust, which converted a viral spike into a sustained player base.
